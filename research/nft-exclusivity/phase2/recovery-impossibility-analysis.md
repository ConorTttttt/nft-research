# What evidence must survive a crash?

Consider two destinations A and B, both authorised by the same seller from version s. Accepting a certificate is irreversible and requires no future online freshness check. Version history is anchored before the choice. Messages can be lost to the recovering party for an unbounded time, including a complete certificate in a buyer's pocket.

**Rollback impossibility.** In execution X the witnesses certify A and a buyer goes offline. Restore every process capable of excluding B, with its keys, to an image predating A. Suppress messages about A. In execution Y no one ever chose A. The recovered signers, authorised seller and new buyer have identical available state in X and Y. Any finite transcript that completes B in Y can be replayed in X, including the same compatible local random choices. Two buyers then have independently acceptable certificates. A protocol that always refuses B can preserve safety at the cost of recovery progress. This argument applies to an open choice with copyable state and keys. Availability of a correct *unrestored* external service or mandatory new verifier checkpoint invalidates the indistinguishability premise. Requiring old buyers to surrender finality changes the contract.

**Hidden certificates and transferable evidence.** A buyer's signature bundle proves that votes existed. It cannot cause a disconnected signer to remember them. Disseminating the assembled bundle is sufficient in some designs; remembering enough individual pre-sign reservations is sufficient here even if assembly happens in private. The write that protects a vote must causally precede releasing the vote. Asynchronous post-sign archival has an execution in which every relevant archive misses the release.

**Clearance needs negative evidence.** After collecting local views, let P be the set of candidates that could have a final certificate in some continuation consistent with the views. Replacing the version by a different fixed successor is safe only if P is empty or the replacement is the same outcome as every still possible certificate. Even P empty at an instant is insufficient if an old live signer can create a new certificate tomorrow. A *permanent fence* supplies information about both the past and future. Reading a quorum of non-binding status statements supplies neither a stop barrier nor complete history.

**Irrecoverable ambiguity.** Take seven, q=5, two identities silent and possibly Byzantine. The five reachable correct reports are A,A,A,B,B. In one history the silent pair signed A. In another they signed nothing. Both present exactly the same closure transcript. A replacement distinct from A is unsafe. With only four accessible reports A,A,B,B and three missing, different fault assignments and messages can make either A or B final in indistinguishable histories. Arbitrary winner selection is unsafe even when the actual history has no certificate. An operator can wait, retrieve the original evidence, or use a protocol that made prepared-state evidence recoverable *before* irreversibility. Starting a consensus election after the fact does not invalidate a hidden bearer certificate.

**Replication bound.** Suppose a signed reservation is deposited in w of N storage processes before a vote escapes, and restart reads r of them. In the simple single-write/quorum-read model, guaranteeing at least one truthful surviving copy requires w+r>N+d, where d is the number of identities allowed to suppress or forget evidence. If the inequality fails, choose the smallest possible intersection and lose it. If it holds, one intersection member can supply the record given authenticated availability. Reconstruct every required reservation, fence concurrent instances and verify completeness. A checksum authenticates the contents of a backup, including an obsolete backup. This intersection lemma alone does not supply a safe snapshot, catch-up protocol or coherent view change.

The lower bounds concern irreversible transferable certificates. Systems that delay acceptance until publication to an agreed log can use that log for recovery. Deployment then inherits the log's finality, admission and availability assumptions. [Castro and Liskov on proactive recovery](https://doi.org/10.1145/571637.571640)
