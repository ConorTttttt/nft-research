# Moving responsibility across disjoint committees

**Prospective handoff theorem.** Every admissible predecessor has a unique authenticated decision policy. Under that policy every two incompatible certified decisions intersect at an identity that cannot equivocate, or another protocol mechanism proves unique selection. The fully final statement fixes the child ID, rights, controller, next admission policy, next roster/threshold and verification domain. Verifiers check the complete path from genesis, and both the old and new domain assumptions hold for their relevant historical periods. Then two different final lineages have a first divergence under exactly one already-authenticated predecessor policy. That policy excludes the divergence. The old/new committee intersection can be empty. This induction extends to a prospective handoff from a predetermined reset child only if its next policy was uniquely fixed in the predecessor; choosing a convenient new roster while redeeming the reset would break uniqueness.

After proof delivery the old group can become unavailable for *future* choices. Copies of the complete handoff must remain retrievable, or a new recipient cannot validate the next group. Losing a privately assembled handoff can strand the asset. The outgoing keys must remain below the historical compromise bound or be securely erased with verifiable epoch discipline. A public identifier called “epoch” does not revoke a certificate that old buyers still accept. New signers need authenticated parent state and unambiguous input IDs. They can give readiness acknowledgements before acceptance if operational liveness matters.

**Retrospective reassignment.** The distributed experiment provisions two disjoint healthy seven-process committees over the same initial right and controller. Each certifies a different recipient using five authentic signatures. A verifier permitting either roster accepts both. A verifier pinned to the original policy rejects the second. For a still-unresolved input one needs the original decision, a sufficiently strong permanent exclusion proof, or a prearranged common reconfiguration authority whose cutover protocol makes old evidence safe. A new committee cannot infer absence of a hidden certificate. The baseline 391-state prospective model still passes. The new processes test the unsafe alternative. Dynamic handoff is outside the executable wire format.

**Selection.** A deterministic hash of a buyer-controlled token ID to participants permits grinding. A VRF or unbiased beacon over fixed eligible identities can reduce predictability; Sybil resistance, seed finality, selection timing and resistance to adaptive capture remain separate assumptions. For uniform sampling without replacement from N eligible parties of which B are corrupt, failure probability is the hypergeometric tail above the budget, conditional on those sampling assumptions. It is positive for many populations and worsens across many attempts. Concentrated infrastructure violates an independence estimate. No permissionless committee-selection guarantee was established. The experiment pins ordered verification keys; a deployment would need an explicit membership authority or independently justified admission system.
