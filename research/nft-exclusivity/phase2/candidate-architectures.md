# Candidate mechanisms and selection

| Mechanism | Finality and recovery | Communication, storage and parallelism | Trust, churn and operational cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEL with a rollback budget and signed closure reports | q durable votes finalise a digest; a negative blocking proof can finalise only the predetermined refund. Some splits remain frozen. | One quorum round for a transfer, one collection phase for a sufficient closure proof plus delivery. O(q) signatures per transfer and up to n signed reports per reset. Retain per-input locks and a retrievable body. Disjoint inputs can proceed independently. | Fixed admitted identities; every lifetime equivocator counts. A larger safety budget costs signatures and persistent replicas. Tested over TCP and in Holochain Wasm. |
| Exclusive-authority resource algebra | Certify a conserving rewrite over explicit extents. Commutativity reduces scope when the frame condition holds. Recovery inherits its underlying arbitration mechanism. | Independent rights can proceed concurrently. A join across domains needs atomic commit or co-location. Encoding declared effects and policy raises verifier cost. | Membership can change only under an authorised rewrite. Incorrect legal decomposition can certify economically incompatible results. Finite model, no general policy VM. |
| Immutable decision program over compatible evidence | Certify a program and the possible evidence worlds once. Exercise locally when authenticated monotone evidence selects a unique child. Repeated exercises yield the same child. | Setup consumes the open-choice right. Presentation can be one-way, with program, evidence and ancestry. No live committee for exercise. Missing evidence may encumber title forever. | A binding commitment, uniquely verified computation over fixed input, or independently trusted non-equivocating oracle supplies compatibility. Open-ended competing oracles move the problem to oracle consensus. |
| Per-domain reviewed BFT state machine | Order spending and recovery under a protocol with prepared-state evidence, persistent safety state and safe view change. Closest match for guaranteed contested progress under eventual synchrony and a correct leader. | Additional phases, proposal dissemination and view-change traffic. Batch/checkpoint to amortise. Shard by independent domains. Cross-shard transactions add coordination. | Still requires admission, state transfer, key lifecycle and bounded faults. An improvised election over Phase 1 locks cannot inherit BFT guarantees. Use a reviewed implementation if contested liveness is a requirement. |
| Hardware or replicated monotonic signing service | A single logical decision register can prevent equivocation. Replacing it requires an authenticated handover and rollback protection. | One logical service call can hide multiple physical replicas. Replication and recovery traffic belong in the cost model. | Device vendor, attestation, recovery root or external quorum becomes a trust dependency. Physical loss or loss of its ledger can halt the right. |

**Evidence-program criterion.** Fix a certified program F, evidence predicates and an admissible-world relation. For any two accepting evidence bundles that can coexist in one world, require equal child ID, controller, extent and policy. This is necessary by presenting both bundles in isolation and sufficient for a single outcome under the evidence assumptions. Example: the seller commits to a sealed bit and a map 0→B, 1→C. Later presentation can designate either recipient across different worlds, with no fresh quorum; binding fixes exactly one world at setup. Another example uses distinct proofs of the same deterministic computation; derive the child from the result, independent of proof encoding. Distinct positive attestations from unrelated oracles can coexist and select incompatible children. A buyer cannot infer mutual exclusion merely from differently named branches. Negative predicates such as “no earlier exercise before noon” require their own trustworthy decision. This generalises predetermined grants while preserving the open-choice lower bound.

Compact Merkle paths and aggregate/threshold signatures are orthogonal encodings. Aggregation changes bytes and verification arithmetic. Threshold signing can add setup, nonce coordination, key refresh and recovery requirements. None reduces the number of independent fault domains needed for intersection.
