# Competing mechanisms

| Candidate and acceptance rule | Conflict and safety mechanism | Network, trust and Holochain fit | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source-chain signature and deterministic fork winner | Two histories can have valid signatures. An arrival-order-independent tie-break can eventually select one after dissemination. No finite observation excludes a hidden branch. | Local write, gossip after partition. Honest peers may accept different owners. | Suitable for discovery and dispute evidence. Fails irreversible acceptance. |
| Replicated ordered state machine | Commit a block or log entry under a BFT protocol, apply only spends of live inputs. Total order rejects the second. | Requires the protocol's quorum, durable locks and liveness assumptions, typically partial synchrony. Minority partitions wait. Can run beside a DNA. | Mature path for heavily contested shared rights or recovery. Rebranding a per-NFT state machine adds deployment burden. |
| Single-slot consistent broadcast (CEL baseline) | Owner signs a parent and successor. Distinct intersecting witnesses persist one choice. A certificate is final even while hidden. | One request/reply phase with enough witnesses. Fixed Byzantine bound, no clocks for safety. Split votes can freeze. Parallel across assets. | Strong baseline for indivisible title. FastPay and Sui already establish closely related design patterns. |
| Independent linear rights with atomic batches | Consume explicit slots for legal effects. Any common input forces intersecting voters. Distinct inputs commute. Bundle digest binds every recipient. | Same-roster, single-controller batch in the prototype. One quorum phase. Needed witnesses can be offline on the other side of a partition. | Selected. Improves workload width and amortised signatures, subject to explicit rights semantics and common fate. |
| Preallocated escrow or conditional release | An initial certificate assigns the only exercise right permanently to B. A hash preimage reveals the fixed child. Two valid releases yield the same child. | After setup one-way delivery to B suffices. B can verify offline with all proof bytes. Failure to reveal traps the asset. Arbitrary next transfers again need certification. | Best when buyer and condition are known in advance. Contradicts a universal claim that every final delivery requires a fresh quorum. |
| Hardware-enforced single writer | An attested non-exportable key signs only after advancing a rollback-resistant counter and binding the parent and recipient. A cloned device state breaks safety. | Local device plus recipient, vendor attestation root, trustworthy hardware and retirement/replacement protocol. Loss can destroy liveness. DHT carries receipts. | Removes online quorum by adding a stronger physical trust assumption. Ordinary Holochain keys do not supply it. |
| Offline electronic cash with blame | Give each merchant a locally verifiable spend transcript. Conflicts can disclose identity or enable slashing on redemption. | Offline acceptance, online redemption, issuer/settlement assumption. A malicious owner can hand out two locally valid receipts. | Useful for bounded financial exposure. Fails the title invariant at offline acceptance. |
| Proof-carrying spent-set accumulator | Prove nonmembership at a prior digest and inclusion at a new digest. Two valid successor roots may extend one digest. | Compact proofs can travel via DHT. Requires an agreed sequence of roots, a trusted sequencer or an honest quorum. | Verification optimisation. Replacing the stamp on a root leaves the choice problem intact. |

Prior art constrains the novelty claim. Asset transfer has weaker agreement requirements than universal consensus in important ownership models. FastPay uses client-driven certification. Sui Lutris addresses owned-object fast paths, shared objects and epoch recovery. Rights decomposition and batching are established techniques. This study contributes a scoped comparison, an exact guard criterion and reproducible Holochain evidence for this design.
