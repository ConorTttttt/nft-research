# Architecture comparison

| Candidate | Economic safety premise | Coordination and failure cost | Applicability |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Native evidence | Authentic funded debit, collected credit, and an authoritative rule excluding every incompatible spend/reclaim across all forks and migrations. | Existing application execution and collection. Availability depends on chain history and dependencies. Standing queries can lag. | Local computation, funding and records are testable. Global exclusion remains unestablished. Abstain on native finality. |
| B. Certified source rights | Canonical debit capacity reserved before it can fund two promises; every spend, cancel, reallocation, recovery and migration consumes that same version. | One source decision for a direct fixed pay/void choice, in addition to title commitment. A separate exclusive reservation needs another decision unless fixed by genesis or coalesced. Split votes can block. | Implemented for a separate laboratory currency with independent signatures. Native adoption requires changed acceptance on all relevant paths. |
| C. Joint commit | Both assets irrevocably prepared for precisely one transaction, then both ledgers recognize one common commit/abort certificate. | Two prepares for independent authorities plus a common decision and recovery. Failure after one prepare can block indefinitely. One common durable registry reduces participants only by changing custody. | Better if both domains will enforce prepared state. Hash-lock swaps need compatible finality, refund ordering and evidence delivery margins. Time alone cannot supply those. |
| D. Ordered clearing / novation | Move both rights into one BFT state machine or solvent regulated escrow and accept its recorded transfer or guarantee as final. A native exit still needs a backing and redemption rule. | Agreement over a log or reliance on one operator. Replicas aid availability; admission, fraud, custody and solvency become first-class assumptions. | Operationally simpler for participants who explicitly accept a clearing claim. No evidence that the native Unyt asset presently delegates all exits to it. |

Select B as a precise **conformance requirement** for unrestricted adversarial transfers. A is preferable if its native implementation can show equivalent exclusion. C is preferable if atomic preparation exists on both sides. No performance ordering across unimplemented candidates was measured. [Gray and Lamport, Transaction Commit](https://doi.org/10.1145/1132863.1132867) · [Herlihy, Atomic Cross-Chain Swaps](https://doi.org/10.1145/3212734.3212736)
