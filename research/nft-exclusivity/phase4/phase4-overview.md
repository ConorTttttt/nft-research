# Phase 4 decision on funded Unyt settlement

**A disposable funded payment worked. Irreversible native settlement remains unverified.** Two separately keyed cells running official Unyt Sandbox v0.117.0 WASMs funded the buyer, parked 10 units, executed an NFT-sale agreement and credited the seller by 10. A CEL witness quorum signed the test grant against the real parked-spend and agreement identities before execution. The buyer's cell retrieved five signed native records. After conductor shutdown an independent verifier authenticated the funding commitment, buyer acceptance, parked-spend link, RAVE and seller receipt. The independent verifier still returns **Pending**. Releasing or refunding the NFT from this evidence would add an unsupported economic assumption. ## Acceptance questions

| Question | Phase 4 answer |
|---|---|
| A. What does the record prove? | With authenticated action data and validated dependencies, it establishes a historical execution and allocation under that DNA and agreement. A receipt and ledger read add evidence of actual collection on a particular history. The signed-record export permits offline authentication of authors and referenced bytes. |
| B. Can reclaim, fork or migration contradict it? | The accessible native contract does not exclude this for every admissible history. We exercised a legitimate reclaim of a separate instrument. The conflicting outcome is a formal counterexample under weaker recognition rules, not an observed release defect. |
| C. What establishes irrevocable payment? | Evidence that authentic, exclusively funded capacity vested exactly the grant's amount and asset irrevocably in the named seller, binding the prepared NFT parent and excluding every incompatible exit across epochs. The native capture lacks that last guarantee. |
| D. Can pay and refund coexist? | Impossible within the restricted certified-source assumptions. Reachable if both authorities can recognize incompatible exits, if lifetime equivocation exceeds the bound, or if a new epoch starts from unresolved state. Native exclusion remains open. |
| E. Executor disappears? | The lab buyer recovers the complete certificate independently from a surviving archive. If a terminal decision existed but its last copy vanished before replication, both parties can stay locked. |
| F. Can every proof be recovered? | Conditional on retaining complete authenticated dependencies, out-of-band policy roots and eventual delivery. A fresh client cannot reconstruct missing signatures from an agreement body. Native economic exclusion evidence has no established issuer in this study. |
| G. Duplicate imports? | Two independent accepting realms can each recognize one. Require a common exclusive source export or a universally pinned acceptance policy with an enforceable source fence. |
| H. Legacy clients? | They can continue circulating and accepting legacy transfers according to their own visible histories. A CEL wrapper can coexist economically with a legacy claim. |
| I. Strongest practical architecture? | Precommit the NFT beneficiary and refund, then interpret one irrevocable disposition of the underlying funded capacity. Prefer a native mechanism if it proves complete conflict coverage. Keep the native adapter Pending meanwhile. |
| J. New trust and coordination? | An authority recognized at every relevant payment exit, durable per-source exclusion, and sufficient archival availability. The lab instantiates two disjoint 7-member rosters, five signatures each. Additional NFT votes after payment would not repair an uncovered native exit. |
| K. Improvement over Phase 3? | Actual stock-WASM funding, parking, execution, collection and second-cell retrieval replace the simulated issuer for the historical path. Separately, the lab discharges the single simulated signer's exclusivity assumption under an explicit quorum assumption, tests withheld evidence and rehearses executor-independent recovery. |
| L. Ready? | Appropriate for isolated disposable funded experiments. Native-backed release, refund or onward transfer presented as irrevocable remains blocked. No production or shared testnet value was used. |

The native experiment creates the grant after it knows the parked-spend action. This avoids a circular hash dependency, but exposes an ordering gap: a buyer may park before obtaining a title lock and the release template itself does not verify that lock. Also, the native experiment uses the Phase 2 reservation primitive and a fixture-bound interpretation of its child. A client accepting plain Phase 2 transfers from that child would bypass conditional semantics. A production deployment must enforce the conditional parent type at every recognized descendant validator, bind the real title owner to the payment principal and use a source-wide import fence. The Phase 3 guarded lab implements its own conditional install rules. No native onward title was issued on the strength of the RAVE.
