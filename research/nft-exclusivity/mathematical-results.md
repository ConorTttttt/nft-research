# Results and limits

## A cut lower bound

Fix one predecessor and two incompatible choices at a common prefix. In the transcript-splicing model in the first-principles analysis, every pair of independently sufficient decision paths must share an unforgeable, consistency-enforcing dependency after the choice, or one of those paths must be unavailable. Merely sharing a stateless verifier or a historical genesis digest is insufficient. This is a necessity argument for the specified copyable-state, open-choice model. It does not give a universal message count for all hardware, synchrony, setup or economic assumptions.

For one round of uniform threshold witnesses, two size-q subsets of n have overlap at least max(0, 2q−n). Safety against any f equivocators requires 2q−n>f. Necessity follows by selecting minimum-overlap sets and corrupting their intersection if the inequality fails. Sufficiency follows because one shared honest witness durably excludes a second statement. For an uncontended correct request to finish despite f silent witnesses, q≤n−f. Together these imply n≥3f+1 and at minimum n, q=2f+1. For f=2, the minimum such roster and quorum are seven and five. A client can fan out to all seven and stop at five valid replies. Distinct failure domains matter more than process count. More generally a quorum family needs an honest intersection for every incompatible pair and an accessible quorum in the complement of every admissible failed set. [Malkhi and Reiter, Byzantine quorum systems](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004460050050)

## Exact coordination scopes

Fix a reachable state and a simple conflict graph G on individually enabled proposals. An edge means they cannot both succeed at that state. Give each proposal a set of exclusive one-use guards. In this representation two proposals conflict precisely when their sets intersect. For each guard, its users form a clique. Every edge must belong to one such clique. Conversely assign one guard per clique in any edge-clique cover. Thus the minimum number of shared guard identities equals the edge-clique cover number, ignoring private guards for isolated vertices. This is the classical graph intersection representation, applied here as a lower bound within this design class. It counts keys, not messages, replicas or minimum latency. [Erdős, Goodman and Pósa](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1966-014-3)

Example: k independent assignments and one sale that consumes all k form a star. No clique covers two spokes. The minimum is k shared guards, each assignment consumes one and the sale consumes all k. A whole-object version introduces k(k−1)/2 false conflicts. Under a workload with all k assignments simultaneously enabled the independent width rises from one to k; a sequential whole-object scheme can still perform all k after rebasing. The same witness roster can serve every guard. On one SQLite connection their physical writes still serialise.

A title sale that *preserves* leases consumes only title. A free-and-clear sale must also consume every encumbrance guard, including the authority to create new leases. Looking up all *known* leases misses a concurrently created one. Genesis must fix a finite complete policy and represent a vacant lease slot explicitly. Pairwise graphs do not capture ternary capacity constraints (any two of three unit draws from capacity two are allowed). Graph covers alone supply neither guard reassignment nor general transaction semantics.

## Batch safety and costs

Fix a canonical statement T consuming m distinct certified inputs in one domain. Every honest voter verifies owner authorisation, reserves every input for digest H(T) in one durable transaction, and signs only after commit. Suppose distinct certified T,U share an input. Their quorums contain an honest common voter, whose durable mapping for that input would have two values. Contradiction. Starting at unique genesis, induction on proof depth gives at most one child per right version. Distinct quorum encodings, publication actions and retry times leave the child ID invariant. Duplicate inputs are excluded before certification. Recipients inherit exactly the named rights; repeated delivery creates no extra right.

For m same-controller, same-roster spends, individual certificates require mq witness signatures. One atomic batch requires q. Both carry Ω(m) output information for arbitrary recipients. With plain Ed25519 and fixed membership the witness signature payload falls from 64mq to 64q bytes, plus indices. Owner signatures fall from m to one. Fan-out need happen only once for the batch, although separate transfers can also pipeline into one RTT or share a transport envelope. A verifier can reuse a common batch; an isolated buyer in this implementation needs all m statements. Common fate, disclosed siblings, assembly delay and stalled co-inputs are explicit costs. A batched CEL baseline can match this gain. The conflict-width gain survives batching only where product semantics really apply.

## Moving a live right to a disjoint roster

There is a limited way to remove persistent *online* witness responsibility for individual assets. Extend each output to bind its next roster. Admit that roster by a publicly verified policy independent of the transferring owner's preference. A handoff is an ordinary exclusive spend of the old version. New witnesses verify the handoff and reserve only its child. By induction the first divergent handoff would require two certificates from the same predecessor roster. Its honest-intersection proof rules that out. Once a recipient has the complete handoff proof, later transfers require zero online participants from the old roster, even if old and new rosters are disjoint. With static corruption bounds for both rosters, no old/new quorum intersection is needed because they decide different input versions.

The change moves responsibility prospectively. It cannot reassign an already locked input without its old quorum, or let a new committee recover unpublished old signatures. Proof retention and protection from retrospective old-key compromise remain necessary. Before accepting the handoff, a recipient may require confirmation that enough new nodes are provisioned to make the child spendable. The executable handoff model tests this extension; the Rust wire format fixes the roster.
